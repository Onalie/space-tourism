Black Carbon & Global Warming

Rocket soot is small in mass but powerful in context, especially when it is deposited high enough to avoid rapid removal.

Mitchell (2009), NASA/GSFC Scientific Visualization Studio
03

Black carbon is a small part of rocket exhaust by mass, but its climate influence can become disproportionately large when it is injected directly into the upper atmosphere.

A global atmospheric modelling study found that black carbon from contemporary rocket activity produces about 3.9 milliwatts per square metre of warming after a decade of emissions. Adding three years of routine space-tourism launches increased the modelled forcing to about 7.9 milliwatts per square metre.

That result is striking because rocket black carbon represented only about 0.02% of emissions from all other black-carbon sources in the model, yet contributed roughly 6% as much global radiative forcing. The reason is altitude: per unit mass, black carbon injected into the upper atmosphere was estimated to be almost 500 times more efficient at warming than soot from surface and aviation sources.

A separate 2022 study explored a much larger future rocket-black-carbon scenario of 10 gigagrams per year. This is not a description of current conditions, but it shows the sensitivity of the stratosphere if emissions expand substantially. The model produced stratospheric warming of up to about 1.5 K, changes in atmospheric circulation and seasonal ozone reductions at high northern latitudes.

Together, the studies show that black carbon is more than a local exhaust plume. It can alter temperature, circulation and ozone chemistry, connecting rocket pollution to several parts of the climate system at once.

Research story

Why altitude turns soot into a larger climate problem.

Black carbon already absorbs solar energy efficiently. Rocket launches intensify the effect by depositing soot in an atmospheric region where it is difficult to remove.

01

Rocket fuel chemistry

Kerosene and hybrid propellants can produce black carbon particles alongside carbon dioxide and water vapour.

02

Long residence time

Stratospheric soot sits largely above the weather systems that wash particles from the lower atmosphere, allowing it to remain for years.

03

Continuous solar absorption

While aloft, dark particles repeatedly absorb incoming sunlight and warm the surrounding atmosphere, creating a persistent rather than momentary influence.

The takeaway.

Black carbon turns altitude into leverage: a small amount of soot can remain aloft for years and produce a warming effect vastly greater than the same amount near the surface.
